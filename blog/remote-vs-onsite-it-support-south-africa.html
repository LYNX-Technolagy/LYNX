Remote vs On-Site IT Support: What Does Your Business Need?

Published 20 November 2026 · LYNX Technology

When something goes wrong with business technology, the first decision is often whether to arrange an on-site visit or ask for remote help. Both approaches have a place. The right option depends on the fault, the equipment involved and whether someone needs to physically inspect or repair it. LYNX Troubleshoot helps businesses understand the next practical step.

What remote IT support can handle

With your permission and a suitable secure connection, a technician can often investigate software and configuration issues without travelling to your premises. Examples include application errors, account settings, operating-system troubleshooting, software updates and some network configuration problems.

  • Software installation and configuration.
  • Email, account and application troubleshooting.
  • Operating-system updates and performance checks.
  • Guidance with backups and basic security settings.
  • Initial diagnosis of some internet and connectivity issues.

When an on-site visit makes more sense

A remote session cannot replace physical inspection. A damaged laptop screen, faulty charging port, loose cable, failed router or printer hardware issue may require someone to examine the equipment directly. On-site support is also useful when several devices or a shared office network need to be checked together.

Security and access matter

Remote support should be transparent and permission-based. You should know who is connecting, why access is needed and what the technician will do. Use trusted remote-support tools, avoid sharing passwords in plain text and end sessions when the work is complete. For sensitive business systems, agree on access controls before work begins.

What should you ask your IT provider?

  • Can you explain what access you need before connecting?
  • Will you ask permission before making changes?
  • What information should we back up first?
  • If remote troubleshooting does not work, what happens next?
  • Are travel or call-out fees separate from the repair cost?

A practical support model for South African businesses

A remote-first process can reduce delays for issues that do not require physical work. Businesses in East London and nearby Eastern Cape communities can also arrange local on-site support when equipment needs hands-on attention. For businesses elsewhere in South Africa, remote support can resolve many software and configuration issues without a site visit.

Choose the approach that solves the problem
