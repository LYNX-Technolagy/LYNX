How to tell if your laptop needs repair or replacement

Published 21 October 2026

A slow laptop is not automatically a broken laptop, and a broken laptop is not automatically a dead one. Most people bring a machine in expecting the worst, and most of the time it's a fixable problem — a failing drive, a clogged fan, or an operating system that just needs a clean install. Signs it's worth repairing

A repair makes sense when the cost is well under half the price of a comparable new machine, and when the rest of the hardware is still healthy.

  • It's slow, not dead. A sluggish machine with a working screen and keyboard is almost always a software or storage problem.
  • The fan is loud or the laptop is hot. That's dust and dried thermal paste — a cheap, fast fix that buys years.
  • It won't boot, but the drive spins up. Often a corrupted OS or a failing drive. Both are replaceable.
  • The battery dies fast. Batteries are consumables. Replacing one on a good laptop is almost always worth it.

Signs it's time to replace

Replacement is the honest answer when the repair cost starts approaching the price of something newer and faster.

  • The motherboard has failed. On older machines, motherboard replacement is often more expensive than the laptop is worth.
  • The screen is cracked and the chassis is warped. When two or three major components fail at once, you're rebuilding, not repairing.
  • It's more than six or seven years old. New low-end laptops now out-perform mid-range machines from a decade ago.
  • It can't run the software you need. Some hardware simply can't be upgraded to meet current requirements.

The honest rule of thumb

If the repair quote is under half the price of a comparable replacement, repair it. If it's over, replace it. If it's somewhere in between, it depends on how much you like the machine and how soon you'd want a new one anyway.

The one thing we'd always push back on is throwing a laptop away without a diagnosis. A fifteen-minute check-up costs a fraction of a new machine, and it's the only way to know which side of the line you're on.
