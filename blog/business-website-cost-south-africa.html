How Much Does a Business Website Cost in South Africa?

Published 20 November 2026 · LYNX Technology

A business website can be a focused five-page introduction or a custom platform with bookings, payments and integrations. Those are very different projects, so there is no single price that fits every South African business. The useful question is not simply what a website costs, but what your business needs it to do. LYNX Full-Stack builds websites and web applications around that scope.

What determines the cost of a website?

The main cost drivers are the amount of design and content work, the number of page templates, the functionality required and the complexity of any integrations. A brochure website with a contact form is usually simpler than an online store, customer portal or booking system.

  • Page count and content: original copy, photography and complex layouts take planning and production time.
  • Design requirements: a tailored design takes more work than adapting a simple established layout.
  • Functionality: forms, bookings, payments, accounts and searchable catalogues add complexity.
  • Integrations: connecting stock, accounting, CRM or third-party systems requires additional development and testing.
  • Ongoing needs: hosting, updates, backups, support and content changes should be considered separately.

What should a website quote include?

A useful quote should describe the deliverables rather than hide everything behind a single number. It should state how many pages or templates are included, who supplies the text and images, which devices and browsers are considered, what functionality is covered, and how revisions are handled.

  • A written scope with included pages and features.
  • A clear split between once-off development and recurring costs.
  • Ownership and access details for the domain, hosting and website files.
  • A delivery timeline with the information or approvals needed from you.
  • A handover plan and an explanation of post-launch support.

Do not pay for features you will not use

A new business may need a credible online presence, clear service pages and a reliable way for customers to enquire. It may not need a complex membership system or custom dashboard on day one. Start with the customer journey that matters most, then add functionality when there is a real business reason.

Remember the ongoing costs

A website is not finished from a business perspective when it goes live. Domain renewal, hosting, email, security updates, backups and content changes may create ongoing costs. Confirm which items are included in the development price and who is responsible for each one.

Compare value, not just the lowest number

Ask each developer to quote against the same brief. Compare the scope, communication, ownership terms, support and likely future costs.
